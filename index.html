The twelve

    How the music was made

    1. SFVFS reads the painting

      A clock of twelve seats and seven rings is laid over each flower, with seat 12 at the top. Every painted mark is found and placed on it: the bead chains in the petals, the dots in the maze, the leaves.

    2. The marks become a tune

      Each seat is a note. A chain of beads starts on its seat's note, then every bend to the right steps the tune up and every bend to the left steps it down. The chains are read in the SFVFS +5 walk: seat 12, 5, 10, 3 and on round. Dots set the speed and the hi-hats, leaves give the bass. Nothing sounds unless a mark in the painting causes it.

    3. This flower's own story

    4. A piece of maths gets a voice

      Each flower was paired with a theorem or open problem chosen from its own numbers. A robot voice speaks about it, its tone pushed through the flower's outline.

    5. Suno makes the final track

      Marc took each source track into Suno, an AI music generator. Suno produced the versions you hear on this jukebox.
